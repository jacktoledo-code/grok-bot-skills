# Florida single-member LLC year

Goal: never miss a state, local, or federal date for a one-person Florida LLC. The agent builds and maintains the calendar, gathers documents, and drafts. The owner files, pays, and signs.

Scope: a Florida LLC with one owner who is an individual, taxed as a disregarded entity. Not for LLCs taxed as S-corps or C-corps, multi-member LLCs, or LLCs owned by another company. Payroll (employees, reemployment tax) is out of scope.

> Not tax or legal advice. Rules and fees change. Confirm on the official sites below or with a professional.

## How the agent runs it

1. **Set up once.** Ask the owner:
   - Date the articles of organization became effective (formation date).
   - Is a fictitious name (DBA) registered? In what year?
   - What does the business sell, and how? Services, digital products, physical goods, rentals; direct, own web store, events, or only through a marketplace.
   - Which Florida county and city is the business based in? Does it own equipment, furniture, or other business property?
   - Will it pay any non-employee contractors?
   - Who prepares the owner's Form 1040?

   Store the answers in a private note, never in shared or public files.
2. **Build the calendar.** Create recurring events, each with reminders 2 weeks and 2 days before, for every date below that applies. Skip what doesn't apply and note why.
3. **Keep a document inbox.** Receipts, invoices, and state, county, or IRS letters go to one inbox folder. The agent files them by category and month.
4. **Monthly check (about 5 minutes).** Report in plain words: new official mail, unfiled receipts, and anything due in the next 30 days.
5. **Draft, don't file.** The agent prepares numbers, pre-fills notes, and opens the right official page. The owner logs in, reviews, signs, and pays. The agent never stores passwords; the owner types credentials.
6. **Log.** After each filing, record the date, the amount paid, and where the confirmation is saved. Keep the confirmation number itself out of shared files.

## Florida (state)

- **Annual report** (Division of Corporations, sunbiz.org): filing window **January 1 to May 1**. LLC fee **$138.75**. Filed after May 1: **$400 late fee**. Still not filed by the **third Friday of September**: the LLC is administratively dissolved at close of business on the fourth Friday. Required every year, even if nothing changed.
- **First annual report** is due January 1 to May 1 of the calendar year **after** the articles of organization became effective. Nothing is due in the formation year.
- Everything on the annual report is public record. Use a business or registered-agent address if you don't want a home address published.
- **No Florida personal income tax.** A disregarded single-member LLC owned by an individual does not file a separate Florida corporate income tax return (F-1120).
- **Fictitious name (DBA):** a registration expires **December 31 of the 5th calendar year**, counting the registration year as year one. Renew between January 1 and December 31 of the expiration year ($50). An expired name cannot be renewed or reinstated; you must register it again.

## Local (county and city), if they apply

- **Local business tax receipt:** many Florida counties and cities require one. Receipts renew by **September 30** each year. Late renewals pay a penalty of 10% for October plus 5% per month, capped at 25%. Check your county tax collector and city.
- **Tangible personal property return (DR-405):** if the business owns equipment, furniture, or other business property, file with the county property appraiser by **April 1**. Filing on time gets an exemption of up to $25,000 of assessed value. After an initial return, a business at or under $25,000 can usually skip later returns until its value goes over $25,000.

## Federal (disregarded single-member LLC)

- **Income tax:** profit or loss on **Schedule C**, self-employment tax on **Schedule SE**, both filed with the owner's **Form 1040**, due **April 15** (next business day if that's a weekend or holiday). **Form 4868** extends the time to file to October 15. It does not extend the time to pay.
- **Estimated tax (Form 1040-ES):** generally required if the owner expects to owe **$1,000 or more** when the return is filed. Usual due dates: April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15 of the next year. A weekend or holiday date moves to the next business day.
- **1099-NEC:** for payments to non-employee contractors in the course of business. Threshold is **$2,000** for payments made after December 31, 2025 (it was $600). It may be adjusted for inflation starting in 2027. Furnish to the recipient and file with the IRS by **January 31** of the next year. Collect a Form W-9 before the first payment. Payments to corporations are generally exempt, except legal fees paid to attorneys. Card and payment-app payments are reported by the payment processor, not by you.
- **BOI (Corporate Transparency Act):** companies created in the US, and their US owners, **do not file** BOI reports. FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule removed the requirement, and a final rule effective August 14, 2026 made it permanent. Only foreign companies registered to do business in a US state still report.

## Florida sales tax (only if it applies)

- Register with the Florida Department of Revenue and file **Form DR-15** only if you make taxable sales or rentals in Florida yourself (direct sales, events, your own web store). Whether a product or service is taxable depends on what it is. Confirm with the Department of Revenue.
- Sales through a **marketplace provider** that has certified it collects and remits Florida tax are handled by the marketplace. Leave those sales off your own return.
- **Due dates:** returns and payments are due on the **1st** and late after the **20th** of the month after each reporting period. If the 20th is a weekend or holiday, the deadline moves to the next business day. **Electronic payments must be initiated by 5:00 p.m. ET on the business day before the 20th.**
- **File every period, even with $0 due.** The minimum late penalty is $50, even when no tax is due.
- **Frequency:** monthly by default. The Department may assign quarterly, semiannual, or annual filing if your yearly tax is low. Use the frequency on your registration.
- On-time electronic filers keep a small collection allowance (2.5% of the first $1,200 of tax due, max $30).

## Yearly calendar (template)

| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 1 | Sunbiz annual report window opens; prep info (from the year after formation) | Agent drafts, owner files |
| Jan 15 | Q4 estimated tax for the prior year (if applicable) | Owner pays |
| Jan 31 | 1099-NEC to contractors paid $2,000+ last year | Agent prepares, owner files |
| Mar 1 | Gather Schedule C numbers; send to preparer | Agent |
| Apr 1 | DR-405 tangible property return (if business owns property) | Agent prepares, owner files |
| Apr 15 | Form 1040 + Schedule C/SE, or Form 4868 with payment; Q1 estimate | Owner / preparer |
| Apr 15 | Annual report not filed yet? Final reminder | Agent |
| May 1 | Annual report deadline ($400 late fee after) | Owner |
| Jun 15, Sep 15 | Estimated tax (if applicable) | Owner pays |
| Sep 30 | Local business tax receipt renewal (if required) | Owner pays |
| By the 19th, each filing period | DR-15 sales tax return (only if registered); initiate e-payment by 5 p.m. ET the business day before the 20th | Agent prepares, owner files |
| Dec (DBA expiration year) | Fictitious name renewal | Owner |

## Rules for the agent

- Never file, pay, sign, or send on the owner's behalf without explicit approval each time.
- Keep IDs (EIN, document numbers, account numbers) in private notes only.
- When a date or fee differs from this skill, trust the official source and flag the change to the owner.
